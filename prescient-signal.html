Which concept should go forward, and why?

Method

Concepts are shown to a profiled sample of your target buyers in a custom survey. Closed questions measure appeal, relevance and purchase intent for each concept; GenAI-powered open-ends probe the reasons behind each score so you learn what to change, not only what to keep.

Method

What will the market bear?

Method

Price-sensitivity and willingness-to-pay questions are built into a custom survey and fielded to a profiled sample of buyers. Results are cross-tabulated by segment so you see where the acceptable range moves and for whom.

Typical deliverable

A price-range readout by segment, presented as a scorecard and an executive-ready summary that states the recommended position and the evidence for it.

Who are the groups inside your market?

Method

A custom survey captures attitudes, needs and behaviours across a broad profiled sample. AI-driven analytics group respondents into distinct segments; open-end coding gives each segment a voice in its own words.

Which products earn their place in the range?

How we protect data quality

A finding is only as good as the respondents behind it. These are the checks that run on every study before a number reaches a slide.

Sampling

Respondents are recruited through panel providers against a profile written for the study: the attributes that define your buyer, self-reported by the respondent, with quotas that keep the sample balanced on the dimensions the analysis will cut by.

Speeder removal

A respondent who finishes far faster than the questionnaire can be read has not answered it. Completion times are compared against the study's expected length and speeders are removed before analysis.

Straightliner removal

Grid questions are checked for respondents who choose the same answer down a whole column regardless of the question. Those patterns are flagged and removed rather than left to flatten the results.

Attention checks

Questions with a single correct answer are placed inside the survey. Failing them means the rest of a respondent's answers are not used.

Open-end coding

Free-text answers are coded into a defined set of themes so that a thousand sentences become quantities that can be cross-tabulated. Nonsense, duplicated and off-topic text is caught here as well.

Cleaned data, and the counts of what was removed at each step, are available with every study.

Industries

Three sectors where the questions are hard enough to need a custom instrument.

Medical devices and pharmaceuticals

Decisions here run through clinicians, patients, payers and procurement, each with a different definition of value. Surveys are profiled to the specific role in the pathway, and open-ends are coded so that clinical language becomes comparable data.

  • Which features drive device preference among the clinicians who choose it?
  • How do patients weigh a treatment's burden against its benefit?
  • What message earns a product a place on a formulary discussion?

Consumer packaged goods

Range, price and pack decisions are made often and reversed expensively. Profiled shopper samples, fast fielding and segment-level cross-tabs turn a launch debate into a measured choice.

  • Which of three concepts should reach the shelf, and for which shopper?
  • Where does the price point move from acceptable to expensive?
  • Which products in the range compete with each other rather than with rivals?

Residential and commercial real estate

Developers and operators commit capital years before the buyer or tenant arrives. Surveys profiled on location, tenure and budget test demand, amenities and positioning before the ground is broken.

  • What will prospective tenants in this market pay, and for which amenities?
  • How do buyers rank location, layout and price when they trade one off against another?
  • Which positioning does a mixed-use scheme need for each audience it serves?
